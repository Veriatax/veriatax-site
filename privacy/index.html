Privacy Policy

Last updated: 27.11.2026

Veriatax Interactive (operated by Sven Martin Laschke, sole proprietor) develops and publishes mobile games and applications. We are committed to protecting your privacy and handling data transparently and responsibly.

This privacy policy applies to our mobile applications and games. This website (veriatax.com) serves informational purposes only, shows no advertising, and uses a cookie-free analytics service that does not create user profiles.

This privacy policy is available in German and English. In case of discrepancies, the German version shall prevail.

1. Information We Collect (Mobile Apps)

The following applies to our Android apps only. For Meter Scout, only the “Meter Scout” subsection applies. The remaining subsections — statistics and crash reports, advertising data and other services — apply to our games only. What happens when you visit this website is described under section 3.

Meter Scout

Meter Scout is an Android app for recording meter readings and checking your monthly instalments. It works without an account, shows no ads and contains no analytics or crash reporting. Meter Scout does not use Firebase, AdMob or Google Play Games.

Your data stays on your device. The app stores properties, meters, readings, notes, tariffs, instalments and settings in a local database on your device, and calculates consumption and costs there. Meter Scout does not send this data to us or to third parties and does not connect to Veriatax servers for that purpose. Storing data on your device is strictly necessary for the features you use (Section 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG).

Android backup and switching devices. If backup is turned on in your Android settings, Android can back up the app’s database to your Google account and restore it on a new device. This backup is an Android and Google feature. We do not run a service for it and have no access to the backup. You can turn it off in your Android settings.

Export and import. A backup file (JSON) or CSV file is only created when you export it yourself. You choose the destination — a folder, cloud storage or an email, for example — using Android’s share feature. The privacy terms of the provider you choose apply there. The app reads imported files only on your device.

Reminders and widget. The app schedules the reading reminder as a local notification on your device and asks for notification permission to do so. So that a scheduled reminder survives a restart, the app registers for the system start event. The optional home screen widget shows the latest reading of the selected meter on your device. No data is transmitted.

Buying Pro. Google Play handles the one-time “Pro” purchase. The app asks Google Play whether you have bought Pro and acknowledges successful purchases there. Google’s purchase library may send technical information to Google when doing so. The app does not collect card or bank details, and we do not receive payment data. For each purchase, Google provides us with order information: order number, time, product, price and taxes, device model, and the country and, depending on the country, the state or region and postal code. We do not receive buyers’ names or email addresses. We use this information to handle purchases, for example refunds (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR), and retain it where it forms part of our tax records (Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR in conjunction with Section 147 of the German Fiscal Code, AO). Google’s privacy policy applies to the payment.

Reviews. After some use, the app may open Google Play’s review dialog once. Google Play decides whether it appears. Reviews are voluntary, are submitted through Google Play and are published under your Google Play profile name. We can see reviews in the Play Console and reply to them. The app learns neither the star rating nor the review text from the dialog.

Links and contact. Links in the app to this website, the Play Store or your email app open that app outside Meter Scout. Section 3 applies when you visit this website. If you email us, you provide the details contained in your message; we use them only to answer your request (Art. 6(1)(b) or (f) GDPR).

Retention and deletion. Your data remains stored until you delete it in the app, clear the app’s data or uninstall the app. Files you have already shared and existing Android backups are not deleted along with it; you manage a backup in your Google account. As we receive no meter data from Meter Scout, there is nothing for us to delete. We keep order information for a Pro purchase for as long as tax law requires, which for accounting vouchers is generally eight years from the end of the calendar year (Section 147(3) AO). Section 7 (your rights) and section 8 (controller) also apply to Meter Scout.

Statistics and crash reports in our games — only with your consent

In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland this collection is switched off by default: it only starts once you explicitly agree on first launch. Outside those regions it may instead be active by default. Either way, you can switch it on and off at any time using the "Statistics & crash reports" toggle in the app's settings.

Usage statistics (Google Firebase Analytics): session duration, which game modes are started, gameplay events, feature usage, app version, device model and operating system version.

Crash reports (Google Firebase Crashlytics): the error message and time of the crash, device and version details and — depending on the app — the call stack of the error (up to 500 characters) plus technical details about the affected program component.

Both services store an identifier assigned by Google that relates to the device and installation (the Firebase Installation ID and the Crashlytics Installation UUID respectively). This identifier is pseudonymous: it contains no information about you personally, but it does allow several reports to be linked to the same app installation. After the app's data is cleared or the app is reinstalled, a new identifier is assigned; earlier reports can then no longer be linked to it. Because this identifier is stored on your device, consent under § 25(1) TDDDG is required for the collection.

We transmit no user identifier: no name, no email address, and we do not set an identifier of our own that would allow your reports to be linked to a person.

A note on how this works: when transmission is switched off, Crashlytics still records crash information locally on your device. If you enable the feature later, those earlier reports are transmitted retroactively. Your consent is in place at that point — but we want to state it explicitly so that "off" is not confused with "nothing is recorded".

Privacy rights for users in the United States

In the United States, privacy laws exist at state level. Depending on your state of residence you may therefore have additional rights — in particular the right to opt out of personal information being shared for personalised advertising. The laws use different terms for this: California speaks of "sale" and "sharing", while Texas and most other states use "sale" and "targeted advertising".

Regardless of these differences, our apps offer a single choice, shown through the Google User Messaging Platform in the states concerned ("My data preferences"). You can choose between "Allow the sale or sharing of my data" and "Don't sell or share my data". The choice can be reopened and changed at any time from the app's settings.

On the term "sale": we receive no payment for passing on personal information. Under the broad definitions used by US state laws, however, serving personalised advertising may already fall under those terms. The wording of the choice follows California's, because it is the broadest and therefore also covers the requirements of the other states.

Right to object under Art. 21 GDPR

Where we process your data on the basis of a legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) — which covers the operation of this website, the font delivery, the analytics and answering your enquiries — you have the right to object to that processing at any time on grounds relating to your particular situation.
